    [0:45] What is in those 415 pages is a schema for research data. Before any class name, the procedure, as a scientist or a conservator would tell it: take a sample, say pigment from the painting; measure it; the instrument records the data in a file; someone draws a conclusion, a date for instance. How an empirical claim comes to be, which is what several talks today were about. [→] In the CRM family, step one is CRMsci: S2, sample taking, removed S13, a sample. [→] Step two: S21, a measurement, measured the sample, admissible because a sample is an observable entity, and observed a dimension: the value. [→] Step three is CRMdig: D11, a digital measurement event, created a data object; and D11 is declared a subclass of S21, in another document. [→] Step four: S5, inference making, is an argumentation, declared in CRMinf, and an argumentation concluded that a belief. Every step of the procedure already has a standard name. I'm not claiming a mapping to the models we heard today: the point is that it exists, and people struggle to read it.

    an analogy · 07Mendeleev, 1869–1871 · simplified: groups I–VII, first five rows

    A periodic table, published as a list of elements.

    Chemists have always kept lists: name, weight, compounds.

    Here, the elements of Mendeleev's 1871 table, ordered by weight, with one oxide each.

    The pattern is already in the list. It does not show.

    Each row becomes a card.

    Still a list: just numbers.

    Arranged by weight and by behaviour, the same cards leave holes.

    Titanium behaves like silicon: it moves right, and a gap opens. Arsenic behaves like phosphorus: two more gaps.

    Colour by group: every column makes the same kind of oxide.

    The last column of the list, now visible as colour.

    And every hole belongs to a group.

    A hole in group III must form R2O3.

    1871: Mendeleev describes the missing elements, weights included.

    Found: gallium 1875, scandium 1879, germanium 1886.

    Gallium's discoverer measured a density of 4.7. Mendeleev, who had never seen the element, wrote that it should be about 6. Purified and remeasured: 5.9.

    The arrangement added no data. It made the gaps visible, and a gap can be tested.

    Weights as in Mendeleev's 1871 table; group VIII omitted. One known oxide per element, “—” where none fits its group; group formulas R2O … R2O7 as in the header of the 1871 table.     Cells, colours and codes as the tool renders them. Grouping classes into colour-coded families comes from the CIDOC CRM Game (Guillem & Bruseker); the anchors, the derivation by inheritance and the provenance stripe are ours.
    [0:50] This is the CIDOC CRM Periodic Table: four documents on one page, 131 classes. At first sight it is chaos; two rules make it readable. Colour follows the hierarchy: seven anchor classes, seven families, and every class inherits the colour of its anchor; grey means no anchor.     [0:40] The obvious objection: why not an ontology editor with a reasoner? Same power, different purpose. An editor is for building and verifying an ontology: its unit of work is the axiom, its question is whether the model is consistent, its users are the designers of the standard.     Adding CRMsci: four small things, and nothing else.

    what it took

    A registry entry, two code-prefix declarations, a data file and a provenance hue.

    a hue chosen by measurement

    Indigo #3A3FA0: 37° of hue from its nearest neighbour among the model and family colours, 8.8:1 contrast on white.

    coloured on arrival

    bonus · how it works · 5paper, Sect. 5

    How a chain crosses documents: read the four as one, then follow the parents.

    document by documentThe chain stops at the border
    CRMsci says: S5 Inference Making ⊑ I1 I1 … not defined in this document

    S5 inherits nothing from I1. The property J2 concluded that never reaches S5; the path S5 → I2 is not found.

    all four documents as one list, keyed by codeThe chain runs to the end
    S5 ⊑ I1 ⊑ E7 ⊑ E5 ⊑ E4 ⊑ E2 ⊑ E1 S5 inherits J2 (from I1) · P14, P16 … (from E7) · P4 (from E2) …

    Every class names only its direct parent. The ancestors are computed once, after the four lists are merged: that is the closure. S5 –J2→ I2 is found.

    no special rule for borders

    A link from CRMsci into CRMinf is a link like any other. That is why a new model costs a registry entry, its prefixes, a data file and a hue.

    the path search

    Bidirectional, subsumption at every hop; at most 4 steps, 600 states per level, 100 results. Truncation is shown.

    “loose”

    A chain whose ends fit the declared domain or range only through a distant superclass: flagged, and hideable.

    For questions on the implementation. The whole design result in a sentence: merge the four models into one dictionary keyed by bare code, then compute the hierarchy closures over the union.
    bonus · scope · 6paper, Sect. 5 · what is verified and what is not

    What it does, and what it doesn't.

    verified

    Entity counts against the official declarations, recomputed by a script shipped with the paper.

    verified

    The transitive subclass closure across model boundaries.

    documented, not scripted

    The path results: each one is a deep link you can re-enter.

    absent by design

    subPropertyOf entailment: a real limit, since CRMsci alone declares 40 subproperty links.

    absent by design

    owl:inverseOf (inverses exist because the RDFS lists them), transitivity, cardinality, quantifiers, shortcuts.

    no consistency checking

    It prevents an inadmissible triple; it does not diagnose one.

    “No path found” is not a proof of absence. An admissible triple says what the schema allows, not that the model is adequate.

    Counts and closure are reproducible from the script and the JSON shipped with the paper; the path results are stated with the deep links that produce them.
    Let me be precise about what this is. Verified: the counts, against the declarations, with a script shipped with the paper; and the subclass closure across models. The demos are deep links: re-enterable, documented, not scripted. [→] Absent by design: subproperty entailment, a real limit since CRMsci alone declares 40; owl:inverseOf, transitivity, cardinality, shortcuts; no consistency checking. [→] So “no path found” is not a proof of absence, and an admissible triple tells you what the schema allows, not that a claim is true or a model adequate.
